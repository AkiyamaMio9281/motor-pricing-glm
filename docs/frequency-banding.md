**Rule, fixed before fitting:** the lowest Poisson deviance on the holdout wins.

## Where linear terms fail

One-way actual over expected claims, out of sample: each specification is
fitted on the training risk groups and scored on the holdout. A specification
that fits leaves every group near 1, within the noise its holdout claim count
implies.

These are holdout figures on purpose. In sample, a GLM reproduces observed
claims exactly within every level of every factor it fits, so any group that
coincides with one of its bands reads 1.000 however good or bad the model is.

## Two ways to place the bands

The plan was equal-exposure deciles. Their first driver band runs from 18 to
29, because drivers under 21 hold 0.72% of exposure, and the first
vehicle band merges age 0 with age 1. Those are exactly the two places
where the linear diagnostics above show risk changing fastest.

The risk-structured bands are narrow where the diagnostics move and wide where
they do not, with bonus-malus 50 and 100 on their own as states of the scale.
They have close to the same number of parameters as the deciles, so the
comparison is about where the boundaries sit rather than how many there are.

## Vehicle age 0

New vehicles have the highest one-way frequency in the book and the shortest exposures, a mean of 0.289 of a year at vehicle age 0 against 0.551 for older vehicles. D2-2 found that short exposures carry more claims per policy-year than an offset allows for, so part of the new-car effect may belong to the exposure relationship rather than to new cars.

| Vehicle age 0 against 1 | All policy-years | Exposure of half a year or more |
|---|---|---|
| raw frequency ratio, 0 against 1 and older | 3.45 | 2.08 |
| GLM relativity, band 0 against band 1 | 3.43 | 2.02 |

Restricting to exposures of half a year or more cuts the modelled relativity from 3.43 to 2.02, which is 43% of it on the log scale. The pricing model is fitted on all policy-years and carries the larger figure. Which one a new car should pay is a rate-table decision, and this is the relativity in the model most exposed to the non-proportionality D2-2 measured.

## Proportionality on the chosen terms

Estimated rather than fixed, the coefficient on log(exposure) under the chosen terms is 0.411, standard error 0.0063. Under the linear terms of D2-2 it was 0.367, so banding does not change the conclusion that expected claims are not proportional to exposure.
