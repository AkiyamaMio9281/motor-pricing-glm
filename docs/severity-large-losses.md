The last column is dominated by single claims: the largest claim, 4,075,401, falls in quintile 5. The rate column is the one to read.

Logistic slope of P(claim above the cap) on log predicted capped severity: 1.016, 95% interval -0.031 to 2.062, an odds ratio of 2.02 for each doubling of predicted severity. With 133 claims above the cap the test has little power, so the interval is the result rather than the p-value.

**The flat load is not rejected by the rule, narrowly.** The interval includes zero, but its lower end is -0.031, and the point estimate doubles the odds of a large claim for each doubling of predicted severity. The rule's verdict stands and a flat load is kept. The evidence leans the other way, and with 133 large claims a concentration of that size could easily escape the test, so the flat load is a provisional simplification for the rate table to revisit, not a finding that large claims fall evenly across the book.
